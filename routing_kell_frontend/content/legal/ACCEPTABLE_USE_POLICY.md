# Kell Acceptable Use Policy

Last updated: October 2026.

Kell runs code-related tasks for you and can change files in your repositories. This policy says what you may not do with it.

## 1. Do not

- use Kell to build, spread or run malware, or to steal credentials, exfiltrate data or damage systems you do not own or have permission to test;
- send material you are not allowed to send, such as another party's confidential data, personal data you have no basis to process, or code you have no right to share with the model providers listed on our [Subprocessors](/docs/legal/subprocessors) page;
- break the law, or infringe anyone's copyright, trademark or other rights (see [Copyright and Third-Party Sources](/docs/legal/copyright));
- try to get around limits, budgets, sign-in, or other safeguards, or to use another person's account or provider key;
- probe, scan or overload Kell, or use it to attack other services;
- reverse engineer the service to build a competing one, or resell access without our written agreement;
- use Kell to produce content that is sexually explicit involving minors, or that harasses, threatens or defames people.

## 2. Teams

If you administer Kell for a company, you are responsible for how the people you invite use it, and for the budgets and provider keys you connect.

## 3. Checks are not a defence

Kell can check results against your tests. A passing check does not make something acceptable under this policy, and a failing screen of our own does not mean we have approved what was not screened. You are responsible for what you ask Kell to do.

## 4. Enforcement

If we think this policy has been broken, we may remove content, stop a task, suspend access, or report unlawful activity to the authorities. We try to give notice first when it is safe to do so.

## 5. Reporting

To report misuse, or something that looks like a security problem, use the contact details at the bottom of this page.
