# Tax notes for the first customers (for a chartered accountant to confirm)

**DRAFT. Not tax advice.** Rates and thresholds change. These notes list the questions to put to an accountant and what I understand today. Confirm each before invoicing.

## 1. Who invoices

| Stage | Who | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Before incorporation | The founder, on their own PAN | Income is the founder's own. A GST registration is needed once turnover passes the threshold, or earlier in some cases, such as supplying services across state lines. Ask the accountant whether registration is needed for the first pilot |
| After incorporation | The company, on its PAN and GST number | Correct from the first invoice. Move invoicing at the Novation Date |

## 2. GST on the Service

- Software-as-a-service for business customers is generally charged **18% GST**. Confirm the classification.
- Place of supply decides whether it is CGST/SGST or IGST. A customer in another state means IGST.
- **A foreign customer:** an export of services can be zero-rated if the conditions are met (payment in convertible foreign exchange, the customer outside India, the supply not in the course of a fixed establishment in India). File a **Letter of Undertaking (LUT)** each year so you can supply without paying tax and claim input credit. Ask whether the Service qualifies.
- Issue a tax invoice with GSTIN, HSN/SAC code and place of supply. File monthly or quarterly returns on time.

## 3. Tax deducted by the customer (TDS)

- Indian customers often deduct tax on payments for software or technical services, commonly under **Section 194J** (fees for technical services) or **194C** (contracts), at rates the law sets. The classification of a SaaS fee has been argued before. Ask the accountant which applies, so the contract's clause ("deducts only as required by law, gives the certificate") works.
- Keep Form 16A certificates. TDS is a credit against the company's tax.
- The Performance Fee follows the same rules as the Platform fee.

## 4. Income tax of the company

- A Private Limited company pays corporate tax at the rate chosen by its regime. Ask which regime suits a loss-making startup.
- **DPIIT startup recognition** may give a three-year income-tax holiday (Section 80-IAC) and relief on carrying forward losses and on some share-issue taxes, subject to conditions and a deadline for incorporation that has been extended before. Check the current rule.
- The "angel tax" on share premium was a concern for startups. Confirm whether it still applies to your issue.

## 5. Payouts to contributors (only if enabled)

- **Section 194O**: an e-commerce operator facilitating sales by participants deducts tax at source on the gross amount. Confirm whether a contributor payout is such a sale. If it is not, a different section (for example for fees or commissions) may apply.
- Collect PAN. Without it, deduction is at a higher rate.
- Payouts abroad: exchange-control rules (FEMA) and withholding tax under the Income-tax Act and any tax treaty. Ask for an authorised-dealer bank's guidance before the first foreign payout.
- GST on the contributor's supply, if they are registered.

## 6. Payments to model providers

- Customer-paid keys (BYOK): the Company pays nothing; no tax issue for us.
- Company-paid model usage for platform-supplied models: the provider is often foreign. Services imported from a foreign provider can attract **GST under reverse charge** and withholding rules. Ask the accountant whether the Company must pay IGST on Vertex or other provider invoices and whether input credit is available.

## 7. Records

Keep invoices, the Calculation Statements for Performance Fees, the provider-call ledger, and the contributor payout records for **8 years**. This matches the retention proposal in the DPA.

## 8. Questions to settle before the first invoice

1. GST registration needed for the pilot, as a founder or after incorporation?
2. SAC code and TDS section for the Platform fee and the Performance Fee.
3. Treatment of a foreign customer (LUT, export).
4. Reverse-charge GST on provider invoices.
5. DPIIT recognition, and when to apply.
6. Whether contributor payouts exist, and the 194O question.
