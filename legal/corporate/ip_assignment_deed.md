## Background

Assignor has, alone or with others, created software and related work for a hosted service that finds proven procedures for AI coding agents and chooses cost-effective models (the **Business**), before the Company was incorporated. The parties wish the Company to own that work.

## 1. Definitions

**Assigned IP** means all intellectual property rights that Assignor owns or may own, anywhere in the world, in the Business and its work product, including:

- source code, object code, scripts, schemas, migrations, tests, prompts, configuration and documentation of the repository **[repository name and URL]**, including the work in `backend/`, `prod_frontend/`, `packaging/`, `experiments/` and `docs/`;
- the routing method, procedure-extraction method, data models and algorithms, whether patentable or not;
- designs, text and graphics, the product names and brand names listed in Schedule 1, and domain names and social accounts listed there;
- datasets, indexes and derived data that Assignor owns;
- inventions and know-how relating to the Business, including patent rights and the right to file applications; and
- the right to sue for past infringement.

**Excluded IP** is listed in Schedule 2 (for example Assignor's prior unrelated work, and third-party and open-source components, which remain under their own licences).

## 2. Assignment

**2.1** In consideration of **[allotment to Assignor of [●] equity shares of ₹[●] each / ₹[●]]** and the Company's promise to carry on the Business, Assignor **assigns absolutely to the Company all right, title and interest in the Assigned IP**, with effect from the date of creation (as far as the law allows) and otherwise from the date of this Deed.

**2.2** For the Copyright Act, 1957: the assignment is **for the full term of copyright, throughout the world, and for all modes of exploitation**, and includes the right to assign further. If this Deed did not say so, the law would limit an assignment to five years and to India. The parties intend neither limit.

**2.3** The Company may modify and combine the Assigned IP without restriction.

## 3. Moral rights

To the extent the law allows, Assignor waives moral rights, including the right to be named as author. Where a right cannot be waived, Assignor will not use it to stop the Company's use of the work.

## 4. Assignor's statements

Assignor states that, to their knowledge:

- (a) they created the Assigned IP, or hold the rights to assign it, and have not assigned or licensed it to anyone else, except as in Schedule 3;
- (b) no employer, university, institute, sponsor or investor has a claim to it, **except as disclosed in Schedule 3**;
- (c) the Assigned IP does not knowingly infringe anyone's rights and contains no third-party code except as listed in Schedule 2 or licence matrix **[legal/corporate/licence_matrix.md]**; and
- (d) they have not put any confidential information of a third party into it.

## 5. Further assurance and power of attorney

Assignor will sign any document and do any act the Company reasonably asks to perfect, register or enforce the Assigned IP, at the Company's cost. Assignor appoints the Company as attorney for that purpose if Assignor does not act within **[15]** days of a written request.

## 6. Return and access

Assignor will hand over to the Company all copies, repositories, credentials and accounts for the Business, and will keep no copy except as Schedule 2 allows.

## 7. Confidentiality

Assignor keeps the non-public parts of the Assigned IP confidential.

## 8. General

- **Law and disputes.** Indian law; sole-arbitrator arbitration in **[Bengaluru / Mumbai / New Delhi]**.
- **Stamp duty.** The Company bears the duty on this Deed and has it stamped under the law of the applicable state. **[Counsel to check the duty payable on assignment of copyright and on the shares issued.]**
- **Registration.** The Company will record the assignment with the Copyright Office or the Patents Office where that helps.
- **Whole agreement.** This Deed is the whole agreement on its subject. Changes in writing.

> **Counsel note:** (1) Confirm the consideration, because an assignment with no stated consideration can be challenged. (2) Check any employer or institute claim: it can defeat the whole assignment. (3) Consider whether a licence-back or a lock-in on the founders' shares is wanted. (4) Consider an assignment of patent rights separately if a filing is planned.
