# Launch on measured proof, not algorithm folklore

[Name] should launch as a compliant, verification-first tool with one number it can defend. Its reach should come from a paid founder account posting native video with no link in the main post, and from people who actually replied. Its conversion should come from a repo anyone can try without signing up, submitted to Show HN and the MCP directories on the same day. About half of the draft X Launch Brief holds up. The strongest supported claims are that **X Premium gives roughly 10x median reach** and that **non-Premium link posts fell to near-zero engagement** after March 2025, both from Buffer's 18.8-million-post study, and that bot-like or AI-sounding replies are now actively suppressed. The weakest claims are the "first 30–60 minutes decide reach" threshold, the "stay in one niche" bonus, the specific reply weights and the "video beats text" ordering. The first three are unverified marketing-blog folklore; the last is contradicted by the only measured comparison, where text beat video on engagement rate. The larger risks are not algorithmic. Anthropic's current terms forbid intermediating Claude usage and using its names in product branding, and it has already suspended a tool author's account (OpenClaw, April 2026). The FTC's standard means the product's most attractive claim, 45–55% cheaper routing, **cannot be published as a result**, because the company's own findings show it is a perfect-check simulation. What the company can say today is narrower and true: in preregistered DS-1000 runs, hook-delivered knowledge lifted open-model coding agents from **52.0% to 60.6% (+8.5 points, p = 0.004)**. The launch pays off only if it turns attention into opted-in, per-goal model evidence. That evidence is the one asset in this category that competitors with 37,000 stars do not have.

## The draft brief gets the direction right and the precision wrong

The evidence base has three tiers, and the grades below depend on which tier a claim rests on. The top tier is primary material from X itself. X re-open-sourced its "For You" ranker as `xai-org/x-algorithm` (the major release covered by TechCrunch on 13 August 2026). The repo confirms that the Phoenix model predicts a separate probability for each action type and combines them in a weighted sum. Those actions are favorites, replies, reposts, quotes, shares, post/profile/link/media clicks, video views, dwell time, active seconds, follows, and negative actions such as blocks, mutes, reports and "not interested". The README states that the weights scale predicted probabilities, not raw counts ([GitHub: xai-org/x-algorithm](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/13/x-open-sources-its-ranking-algorithm-letting-users-see-if-theyve-been-shadowbanned/)). The README does **not** print the numeric weight table. The circulating figures ("reply = 13.5× a like", "report = −234") contradict one another across blogs, and nobody traced them to the config files ([Postory](https://postory.io/blog/x-algorithm-2026); [OpenTweet](https://opentweet.io/blog/how-twitter-x-algorithm-works-2026)). The middle tier is measured work, and here it is essentially one source: Buffer's analysis of 18.8M posts from 71,000 accounts through August 2025. The bottom tier is SEO content, which repeats confident numbers without methodology. Where the brief rests on that tier, it is grading folklore.

## Reach comes from paid accounts and real replies; conversion comes from a try-it-now repo

Premium is the one lever with measured size. Buffer's data shows **roughly a 10x gap in median impressions** between regular and Premium accounts, with Premium+ higher still. Engagement *rates* barely differ between tiers (≈0.49–0.55%), while more than half of regular-account posts drew zero interactions ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/x-premium-review/)). Premium buys distribution, not persuasion. Replies from Premium accounts are also widely reported to rank higher inside threads, but that tier ordering has no measured magnitude. The practical rule is that both founders should hold Premium, and the launch should post from the founder's account, where people expect a person to answer. The brand account should repost. The ranker's negative signals (blocks, mutes, reports, "not interested") are explicit prediction targets in the published code. That makes engagement pods, templated replies and giveaway mechanics a net risk: they trade a few positive signals for negative ones, and X's February 2026 API change shows the platform is actively hunting that pattern.

Conversion runs on different mechanics. The launches that grew fastest did not win on production value. Astral announced uv with one plain sentence: "an extremely fast Python package installer and resolver, written in Rust… a drop-in replacement for pip". It carried no video or thread, and the repo passed 84,000 stars ([Astral on X](https://x.com/astral_sh/status/1758252944409956675); [Astral](https://astral.sh/blog/uv-unified-python-packaging)). The claim was true and anyone could check it in a minute. Cline grew to **5M installs and 57,000 stars** and credits "developers [who] told other developers about something that actually worked" ([Cline](https://cline.ghost.io/5m-installs-1m-open-source-grant-program/)). The closest comparable, claude-code-router, grew steadily from ~25k to **~37.3k stars through 2026** on a pure cost pitch, with no documented viral moment ([HelloGitHub](https://hellogithub.com/en/repository/musistudio/claude-code-router)). OpenCode's star count "went vertical" only after a *forcing event*: Anthropic's January 2026 block on third-party OAuth, followed by legal demands that made OpenCode strip its Claude Pro/Max references ([Saiyam Pathak](https://saiyampathak.substack.com/p/opencode-just-overtook-claude-code)). The evidence for this category points to an X launch acting as an ignition point for utility-driven word of mouth, not as the growth engine itself. Treat the case evidence as thin. No launch-post metrics were recoverable for claude-code-router, ccusage or Bolt.new, whose launch-tweet details sit behind a paywall ([Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/boltnew-growth-journey)).

The conversion surface must match what each channel rewards. Show HN's rules require something people can try, "ideally without barriers such as signups or emails". They exclude landing pages, and they now warn: "Don't post quickly-generated one-offs; anybody can do that now" ([Show HN guidelines](https://news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html)). So submit the GitHub repo URL, which HN treats better than branded URLs ([Star-History](https://www.star-history.com/blog/playbook-for-more-github-stars/)). Use a plain "Show HN: [Name] – what it does" title, and post a first-person first comment right away ([Syften](https://syften.com/blog/hacker-news-marketing/)). The local path must work with nothing but the user's own keys. HN's standing objections to routers are that difficulty "depends a lot on what information is retrievable by the agent" and that model churn makes manual routing futile ([HN thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49126630)). Those objections are the launch's opening. [Name] does not guess difficulty up front. It tries a cheaper model, checks the result and escalates when the check fails, and it can show why each step went where it did. The first comment should say exactly that. It should also admit that checks are weaker than tests, since HN will point it out anyway.

MCP directories are the durable, low-effort channel. The official MCP Registry holds metadata published under a DNS-verified namespace. It is still in preview and may reset data ([MCP Registry](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/registry/quickstart)). Anthropic's Connectors Directory accepts **only remote HTTPS servers**; local components must ship as a Claude Code plugin ([Claude submission](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/building/submission)). It requires a `title` plus a `readOnlyHint` or `destructiveHint` annotation on every tool, OAuth for account-bound tools, and a privacy policy, which it calls grounds for "immediate rejection" if missing. It also requires a documentation URL, a support contact, an icon, a populated reviewer test account and seven policy acknowledgments. A listing starts as "Community" after an automated scan and can later earn "Verified". Among third-party directories, a single source reports that Glama tracks ~37,000 servers, that Smithery actually hosts servers and that PulseMCP pairs its listing with a newsletter ([Tallyfy](https://tallyfy.com/how-to-list-mcp-server-registry-smithery-glama-pulsemcp)). The research notes flag the MCP server's `--workers 1` ceiling as a launch-spike risk. That flag is **out of date**: the v1 transport became stateless on 2026-09-28 (commit 55dab44). The remaining operational job is a load test (ramp, 10x burst, soak) with stubbed judges, plus a status page to absorb "is it down" traffic ([Zero To Mastery](https://zerotomastery.io/blog/how-to-handle-traffic-spikes/); [Flowtriq](https://flowtriq.com/blog/status-pages-kill-ticket-storm)).

## One pooled credential or one unsupported savings number could end the launch

The terms that matter are current and explicit. Anthropic's Claude Code legal page says: "Customers may not pay for, resell, or intermediate Claude usage on their end users' behalf. Each end user must authenticate with their own Anthropic API key, Claude subscription plan credentials, or 3P inference provider credential." It adds that third parties may not offer Claude.ai login or "route requests through Free, Pro, or Max plan credentials on behalf of their users", nor "collect, store, or intermediate Claude.ai credentials or session tokens". Anthropic "may [enforce] without prior notice" ([Anthropic Legal & Compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)). The same page explicitly permits an API key configured for the key owner's own users and billed to that owner. That is the BYOK lane. Anthropic's software directory terms add that a listing grants no trademark rights and forbids implying partnership or endorsement ([Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/13145338-anthropic-software-directory-terms)).

Enforcement has already happened three times in this product's neighbourhood. Anthropic cut Windsurf's direct Claude access with under a week's notice in June 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/07/week-in-review-why-anthropic-cut-access-to-windsurf/)). It formalised the ban on subscription OAuth in third-party tools on 19–20 February 2026 ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/20/anthropic_clarifies_ban_third_party_claude_access/)). And it temporarily suspended the Claude account of OpenClaw's creator in April 2026. Its reason was that subscription plans are priced for interactive use, and OpenClaw users were getting what amounted to $1,000–$5,000/month of API-equivalent usage from a ~$200 plan ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/10/anthropic-temporarily-banned-openclaws-creator-from-accessing-claude/)). The reaction was fierce, but most of it reaches us through a Medium roundup and was not independently verified ([Medium](https://medium.com/@stawils/the-openclaw-ban-that-exposed-anthropics-real-problem-fe8f10aa0e80)). The strategic reading is that backlash events send traffic to *compliant* alternatives. OpenCode benefited only after removing the offending integration. OpenAI's naming rule bars "GPT" in app, product or company names and is actively enforced by message ([Slator](https://slator.com/openai-hunts-down-companies-using-trademarked-gpt-in-brand/)). OpenAI's stance on pooling is documented only through a secondary explainer ([explainx](https://www.explainx.ai/blog/codex-usage-limits-sub2api-sign-in-chatgpt-august-2026)), and no OpenAI enforcement incident against a delegation tool was found.

The product has to be shaped to those rules, not just its copy. First, every provider call uses a key or login belonging to the person running it, configured on their machine or in their own secrets store, and [Name] never sees, stores or relays a Claude.ai or ChatGPT session token. Second, "handing work to other agents" needs care. Running the user's *own* Claude Code or Codex CLI under their own login on their own machine is the same person using their own plan. A hosted service that drives subscription CLIs around the clock is the OpenClaw pattern and must not ship. Where delegation targets Claude or Codex at volume, default to the user's own API key and document why. This line is a judgement, not a sourced ruling, so get counsel to confirm it. Third, delegation *to* free and cheap third-party models carries its own disclosure duty. Free tiers have tight limits (OpenRouter reportedly allows 50 requests/day for new users), and some users knowingly accept "being part of that training set" ([aggregated OpenRouter discussion](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/@DawnReaverWOWS/TheFinalDiscordMCP/blob/5e7433d0fcc21be9c3899f4ea3f996ae94b88cd8/context/openrouterAiModels.md); [itch.io](https://itch.io/post/12984371)). The README and the privacy policy must state which code leaves the machine, to which provider, under whose key, and whether that provider trains on it. Nothing may suggest opening multiple free accounts to stack limits.

Copy is where FTC exposure lives. The FTC judges the "net impression" of the whole ad, including the product name and the charts ([Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2024/03/how-to-substantiate-advertising-claims)). It requires substantiation before publication, and its August 2025 Workado order barred efficacy claims without "competent and reliable evidence" after a "98 percent accurate" detector tested at about 53% ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/08/ftc-approves-final-order-against-workado-llc-which-misrepresented-accuracy-its-artificial)). Set the company's own findings (`docs/findings.md`) against that standard. Only one headline result survives. The hook's automatic knowledge delivery lifted open-model agents from **52.0% to 60.6% (+8.5 points, 95% CI +2.7 to +14.4, p = 0.004, 246 task–model pairs)**; this was preregistered and confirmed. Letting the model decide when to ask was not significant (+3.3 and +4.1). Sonnet gained **+1.2 (CI −5.0 to +7.7)**, which is no measurable effect. The routing result is **89–93% solved versus Sonnet's 82% at 45–55% lower cost**, but it replays past results through a ladder that assumes a perfect check, and it uses placeholder open-model prices. The findings file itself calls it an upper bound. Competitors already advertise figures like LiteLLM's "as much as 56%" ([Medium](https://medium.com/@adnanmasood/portkey-vs-litellm-routing-fallbacks-cost-tracking-and-control-the-llm-gateway-playbook-part-195855dc25c3)), so the temptation to match them will be strong. Resist it until real sessions produce the number.

|---|---|

The plan keeps the brief's arc but changes the substance of each stage. Pre-launch becomes a readiness gate rather than a hype campaign. Launch day becomes a launch week. And the weekly report becomes an evidence report built from opted-in runs instead of commentary on vendors' free tiers.

Three changes to the brief's content pillars follow from the risk section. **Proof** stays the lead pillar and becomes the only one that can carry numbers. The **Leaderboard** becomes a per-goal evidence table, "for pandas reshaping steps, open model X passed the test check 41 of 50 times", rather than a brand-versus-brand ranking. That keeps it factual, substantiated and hard to read as an attack. Refer to vendors in plain text and have counsel check it against Anthropic's trademark guidelines, which the research did not fetch. The weekly report publishes the company's own measurements rather than vendors' free-tier terms, which change without notice and invite "how to game limits" framing. **Build in public** continues, justified by its cost rather than by any growth claim.

On timing, 8:00–8:30 pm IST falls inside the cited 6–9 pm Indian peak. Until US daylight time ends on **1 November 2026**, it lands at about 10:30–11 am US Eastern and 7:30–8 am Pacific. After that date the same IST slot is an hour earlier in the US, about 9:30 am Eastern. If the launch slips past 1 November, the US-morning overlap still holds. The timing sources are posting-tool vendors, so treat them as a starting guess and check the account's own analytics after T-21. HN publishes no official best time. The constraint that does bind is Show HN's requirement that the maker be available, which argues for posting both channels together and staying up.

### Video specs and a toolchain that only uses real sessions

Two video findings from the evidence matter here. The ranker optimises for attention signals, and most social video plays muted: one video vendor puts it at up to 85% ([clicks.video](https://www.clicks.video/blog/silent-first-editing-captions-text-overlays-and-visual-hooks-for-sound-off-viewing)). So the first three seconds must land with the sound off. Split-screen before/after suits a "cheap model tried, check failed, escalated, passed" story ([Think Branded Media](https://thinkbrandedmedia.com/blog/creating-impactful-split-screen-videos-strategies-for-better-click-through-rates/)). The conversion-lift statistics that circulate (4.8% versus 2.9%, 70–80% completion) come from video-tool vendors and should not steer decisions.

The pipeline is sound as specified. Claude drives Remotion through the official plugin (`claude plugin marketplace add remotion-dev/claude-code-plugin`) or `npx remotion skills add`, and `@remotion/captions` normalises caption timing data ([Remotion plugin](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/ai/claude-code-plugin); [captions API](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/captions/api)). One integrity point needs care. VHS *re-types* commands, so "from real session logs only" means either replaying real recorded output (asciinema plus `agg`, [asciinema](https://docs.asciinema.org/manual/agg/)) or re-running the real commands against the real repo. It never means scripting output that did not happen. Every on-screen number should trace to a stored run artefact, and every log must pass through the repo's redaction path first, since `.claude/traces/` holds real transcript data and is local-only.

Remotion licensing is headcount-based. The **Free License covers organisations of up to 3 people**, and commercial use is allowed. At 4 or more people a company licence is needed: "Creators" at **$25 per month per person** writing Remotion code, including through agents, or "Automators" at **$0.01 per render with a $100 monthly minimum** for programmatic pipelines. The two combine under a shared $100 minimum ([Remotion License FAQ](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/license/faq)). A third-party claim that pricing is tiered by ARR conflicts with the official FAQ and should be ignored. If the team crosses three people and renders mostly in CI, the Automators plan fits. Revideo is the fallback, reported as MIT-licensed with a headless `renderVideo()` API; check its LICENSE file first ([snapcn](https://snapcn.dev/remotion-vs-motion-canvas)).

## The launch pays off only if attention becomes per-goal evidence

Stars are the loudest output of a launch and the weakest signal. They are cumulative and never decay, and developers star to bookmark as often as to use ([Rushi's](https://www.rushis.com/github-stars-what-they-measure-and-what-they-do-not/)). Stronger intent shows in opened integration issues, forks and PRs ([LeadCognition](https://leadcognition.io/blog/github-activity-buying-signal/)). Package downloads are "an external distribution metric, not the funnel denominator". The funnel that means something is **install → activated → first_success → d7_retained**, measured with opt-in telemetry and never reported as total users ([telemetry reference](https://github.com/liush2yuxjtu/telemetry)). For [Name], activated means the first delegated step. First success means the first delegated step that *passed a check and was accepted*. That definition ties retention to the product's actual promise.

The distinctive benefit is the data. The repo already has the plumbing: `report_model_run` outcomes feed a per-goal recommender, and procedures and evidence carry scope and provenance (`docs/findings.md`; `backend/app/execution/evidence.py`). Each opted-in delegation leaves a record of which model, on which goal type, under which check, passed or failed, at what cost. Router competitors route on price and static rules. claude-code-router's own motivation document says "the primary reason people use it is cost" ([claude-code-router](https://github.com/musistudio/claude-code-router/blob/main/blog/en/project-motivation-and-how-it-works.md)), and it has no verified outcome record. The finding that "12 of Sonnet's 15 failures were solved by some open model" is exactly the kind of fact only this record can surface at scale. The weekly Delegation Report is that moat's public face. It turns private evidence into public credibility without giving away the raw corpus, and it creates a reason to opt in: contributors can see their runs moved the table. Consent must be real. Opt-in must default to off, scopes must stay visible, and the design should already follow India's DPDP duties, due 13 May 2027 with a possible earlier date, and GDPR from the first targeted EU user.

The revenue path has precedent but little evidence on timing, so treat it as a hypothesis to test. Kilo Code paired a free tier with BYOK "at zero markup" and was acquired by Anaconda in July 2026 ([kilo.ai](https://kilo.ai/); [theaiagentindex](https://theaiagentindex.com/compare/cline-vs-kilo-code)). Cline raised $27M on helping developers control AI spend ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/rashishrivastava/2025/07/31/cline-has-raised-27-million-to-help-developers-control-their-ai-spend/)). Neither shows *when* to charge. The consistent route is to keep inference strictly BYOK with no markup, which also keeps the product outside the "intermediate usage" ban. The paid layer is then a hosted team tier: org-private procedure memory, shared per-goal evidence inside the org, SSO, audit logs of tool calls, admin revocation and a data-layer read-only mode. Enterprise buyers ask for exactly these. Design partners bridge the launch to that tier. The pattern is **5–15 hand-picked teams** trading roadmap influence for preferential pricing ([Koji](https://www.koji.so/docs/design-partner-program)), recruited from teams of 10+ engineers already running coding agents ([px0](https://px0.ai/design-partners/)) and from inbound launch conversations. Their tasks also supply the real-session routing study the savings claim is waiting for. Contributors are the other compounding asset. Cline grew its contributor base 4,704% year on year and hired "many" of its 35+ employees from early contributors.

## Conclusion

The research reverses the brief's priorities. The brief treats the X algorithm as the main variable. The evidence says the algorithm rewards a few cheap, knowable things (a paid account, native media, no bare link, real replies), and that growth in this exact category has come from utility, word of mouth and vendor forcing events, not from launch-post craft. The scarce resource is not reach but **credibility under scrutiny**. HN is already sceptical of routers. Anthropic has shown it will cut off tools that look like pooled access. The FTC now punishes accuracy claims that fail testing. A verification-first product is the rare entrant whose honest pitch is also its best one: it can say exactly what was checked, what passed and what it does not yet know.

That makes the real launch milestone the first **real-session routing number**, not T-0. Until it exists, [Name] should launch loudly on what is true: a preregistered +8.5-point gain for open models, checks that reject bad work, keys that stay with the user. It should then let each week's opted-in evidence build the claim that competitors cannot copy. If a provider tightens limits again, the company that is BYOK-clean, trademark-clean and holding measured per-goal data will get the OpenCode-style wave. A company that has to issue a correction will not.
