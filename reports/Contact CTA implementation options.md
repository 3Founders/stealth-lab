# Ship a Turnstile-gated contact endpoint first

**Build a small own endpoint behind a /contact page: a short form, Turnstile verified server-side by your FastAPI backend, the lead stored as a database row, and a plain-text notification to a shared mailbox or Slack.** Pair "Get started" (filled, primary) with "Contact sales" (outlined, secondary) in the hero, and keep the administrator sign-in as a small text link in the header or footer, never a hero button. Of the options researched, this path adds the fewest new processors (only an email sender) and the least lock-in, and it reuses the Turnstile and FastAPI you already run. It also avoids the weakness of the cheaper alternatives: a bare mailto: link cannot be spam-protected, and hosted form tools are mostly US-hosted and rarely confirmed to support Turnstile. Booking pages and CRMs are real options later, but they are overkill for a team of two to five with no sales process yet. Caveat up front: the evidence base is thin. Several key pages could not be fetched, no independent study of "Contact now" wording exists, and the ranking of the self-built designs is the researchers' inference, not a sourced finding. Details are in the final section on what could not be verified.

## The hero pair: one filled primary, one quieter "Contact sales"

Design systems agree on one primary-styled button per section, a visually lighter secondary, and the primary on the left when two sit side by side ([BlackRock](https://www.blackrock.com/design/nested-components/buttons), [Morningstar](https://design.morningstar.com/systems/marketing/business-website/components/buttons), [SaaSframe](https://www.saasframe.io/best-practices/cta-hierarchy)). Peers follow the same pattern. Vercel's hero reads "Deploy now" and "Talk to sales" ([vercel.com](https://vercel.com)), and Cursor's hero carries "Get started" and "Request a demo" while its header holds "Sign in" and "Contact sales" ([cursor.com](https://cursor.com)). PostHog's hero has only "Get started – free", with "Talk to a human" in a side menu ([posthog.com](https://posthog.com)). Linear has no hero button and puts "Contact sales" beside "Get started" in its closing section ([linear.app](https://linear.app)). That is two of four verified peers with a sales action next to the start action in the hero, so your pairing is conventional rather than risky.

On wording, I recommend replacing "Contact now" with "Contact sales" or "Talk to us". "Contact now" implies urgency without saying who the visitor reaches, and every peer reviewed used a more specific label. The supporting numbers are weak: one vendor reports demo-intent labels converting at 13.1% versus 4.8% for generic "Contact Us" ([Docket](https://www.docket.io/blog/increase-demo-requests-on-your-b2b-website)), and another single example shows 5.1% versus 12.4% ([aitom.cz](https://www.aitom.cz/en/slovnik/call-to-action-cta-vyzva-ktera-v-b2b-rozhoduje-o-konverzi)). Both are the vendors' own unverified data. There is a counter-view that generic "Contact us" suits visitors who are not ready for a demo, but the stronger and better-argued point is that a label must match what happens on click: "Book a demo" should land on a booking flow, and "talk to sales" is clearer than "contact us" about the next step ([Blend B2B](https://www.blendb2b.com/websites-decoded/improve-website-conversion-rate)). The rule that follows is to name the button after the destination: a form gets "Contact sales", a calendar gets "Book a demo". Since you are not recommending a calendar yet, use "Contact sales".

For layout, WCAG 2.2 requires pointer targets of at least 24x24 CSS px at AA ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)), and 44x44 at AAA ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-enhanced.html)). The sensible reading is to aim for 44px-tall buttons, stack them full-width on narrow screens with the primary on top, and leave a visible gap.

## Where the administrator sign-in belongs

No peer reviewed shows a separate administrator link on its marketing site; Cursor's header has "Sign in" and Linear's has "Log in" and "Open app", with no distinct operator login in the content retrieved ([cursor.com](https://cursor.com), [linear.app](https://linear.app)). So this placement is design judgement, not research. The recommendation is a low-emphasis text link, "Administrator sign in", in the footer and optionally the header, far from the hero buttons so it cannot be mistaken for "Get started". If it stays as small inline text it relies on WCAG's inline and spacing exceptions, which is another reason to keep it clear of the buttons. Your site already uses this wording on /sign-in and in the footer, which is consistent with the advice.

## Why an own endpoint beats mailto, hosted forms and booking pages

The central design move is to make the button a plain link to your own /contact route, so that whatever sits behind it can be swapped without touching the hero. Lock-in ranks, from lowest to highest, as mailto, own endpoint, hosted form (change the POST URL), Cal.com or Calendly (links and calendar data), then HubSpot, Plain or Crisp (accumulated contacts and workflows). That ranking is the researchers' inference, with no source comparing migration effort.

Mailto needs no vendor but cannot host Turnstile, which requires a server POST to siteverify with the secret and the token ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/turnstile/get-started/server-side-validation/)). It also captures nothing for visitors without a configured mail client and publishes an address for scrapers. It is a reasonable stopgap link in the footer, not the main path.

Hosted forms are cheap but do not fit your stack cleanly. Formspree's free tier is 50 submissions a month and its plans page does not mention Turnstile; Slack is included even on Free ([Formspree](https://formspree.io/plans)). Basin's free tier (1 form, 50 submissions, 30-day retention) does offer Turnstile, but Slack needs a paid plan ([Basin](https://docs.usebasin.com/plan-comparison/)). Web3Forms is reported US-hosted, publishes a DPA and says it forwards rather than stores submissions ([DPA](https://web3forms.com/dpa), [FAQ](https://docs.web3forms.com/getting-started/faq)), but its Turnstile support appears to be a paid tier per third parties only. Tally is free, Belgium-based and states that it stores data in Europe ([Tally](https://tally.so/pricing)), which makes it the strongest hosted option on compliance, though whether it works with your Turnstile widget was not checked. Each hosted form adds a new data processor and a DPA review, which is the compliance cost a small team should avoid when it can.

Booking pages suit buyers who already want a meeting, not a first contact. Cal.com's free plan is single-user, and a shared team inbox needs Teams at $12 per user per month ([Cal.com](https://cal.com/pricing)). Its public repository now presents a self-hosted community fork with no enterprise code, so self-hosting is murky ([GitHub](https://github.com/calcom/cal.com)). Calendly's free plan allows one event type and one-on-one meetings only ([Calendly](https://calendly.com/pricing)). Neither carries a free-text problem statement by default or your Turnstile. CRMs and helpdesks are heavier than the problem: HubSpot's free CRM allows 2 users and 1,000 contacts ([HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/crm)), Plain starts at $45 per user per month with no permanent free plan ([Plain](https://www.plain.com/pricing)), and Crisp's free plan has 2 agents ([Crisp](https://www.crisp.chat/en/pricing/)). Revisit HubSpot's free tier when leads need follow-up tracking beyond a status column.

On the own-endpoint design, the notes compare three: A, a Next route or serverless function that only emails; B, an Edge Function plus table plus Supabase database webhook; and C, a FastAPI endpoint that verifies Turnstile, inserts the lead and notifies. C is the best fit because the Turnstile secret, database credentials and email key stay on one server you already operate, with normal tests, logs and rate limiting. B needs an Edge Function in front anyway to verify Turnstile, and Supabase's webhook page states no delivery guarantee or retry behaviour ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/database/webhooks)). A has no durable store if the email provider fails. The notes also describe a Next route handler as an under-a-day option, which works because your site runs a normal Next deployment, not `output: 'export'` ([Next.js](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/building-your-application/deploying/static-exports)); but a Next route would split secrets across two servers, so I prefer FastAPI, with the Next page posting to it. For email, Resend's free tier of 100 emails a day and 3,000 a month is ample for contact volume ([Resend](https://resend.com/docs/knowledge-base/account-quotas-and-limits)).

## The form and the security checklist

Keep the form to a work email, a name, and one free-text "what do you need" field, with company optional. Baymard's checkout research finds field count matters more to usability than step count ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/checkout-flow-average-form-fields)); it is not B2B contact research, but Supabase's own sales form is similarly short (first name, last name, company email, "what are you interested in?", plus a consent line) ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/contact/sales)). State the next step beside the button, and give a reply time only if the team can meet it, because no peer page retrieved published one.

For security, Turnstile must be verified server-side: the widget alone protects nothing, and only your backend should call siteverify because an exposed secret lets attackers bypass the check ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/turnstile/get-started/server-side-validation/)). Tokens live 300 seconds, are single-use (replays return `timeout-or-duplicate`), and the response carries `hostname` and `action`, which you should compare yourself, using short timeouts and an optional idempotency key. The notes' synthesized checklist of common mistakes is worth adopting as the build spec: do not fail open when siteverify times out; keep the Turnstile secret and any service-role key out of NEXT_PUBLIC variables; if the lead goes in a Supabase table, enable RLS with no anon read, because a table without RLS is readable and writable with the anon key ([Supabase RLS](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/row-level-security), seen only via search summary), and avoid blanket `with check (true)` insert policies. Writing through FastAPI with a server credential sidesteps most of this. For email, use a fixed From on your verified domain, a fixed To, and the visitor's address only in Reply-To, stripping CR/LF from names and subjects, since unvalidated input in headers lets an attacker add Bcc recipients and turn the form into a spam relay ([Beagle Security](https://beaglesecurity.com/blog/vulnerability/email-header-injection.html)). Send plain text, not HTML, into email and Slack. Add per-IP and per-email rate limits, a body-size cap and field-length limits, a honeypot field, and no automatic confirmation email to the supplied address, which would make you a mail-bomb relay. Do not log full payloads. These last items are the researchers' recommendations without a fetched source.

## Privacy: short notice, fixed retention, signed processor terms

The notes treat an enquiry as legitimate-interests or pre-contract processing, but this rests on third-party examples, since council notices vary between consent, public task and legitimate interests, and no regulator guidance was retrieved. The practical minimum is a notice beside the submit button saying what is collected (name, email, message), why, which processors handle it (your database, the email provider, Slack if used), how long you keep it, and how to request access, correction or deletion. Published examples keep enquiries anywhere from until resolved, to 30 days, to 2 years after last contact ([East Sussex](https://eastsussex.gov.uk/privacy/email-us), [Bath & NE Somerset](https://www.bathnes.gov.uk/council-privacy-notices/business-contacts-privacy-notice)); the notes suggest 12 months or less after last message, a decision for you, not a sourced rule. Implement it with a scheduled delete job and deletion by email on request, and remember Slack holds a copy too, so notify with a summary and link to the lead rather than the full message. Accept the DPA of each processor; Resend's, for instance, covers EU SCCs, a UK addendum and the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, effective on accepting its Terms ([Resend DPA](https://resend.com/legal/dpa)). Collect no marketing consent here, and do not store full IPs beyond abuse needs.

## Recommended path and build order

Recommended: a /contact page on the Next site, posting to a FastAPI endpoint that verifies Turnstile, writes a lead row (source of truth), and sends a best-effort plain-text notification via Resend to a shared team alias, with hero buttons "Get started" and "Contact sales" and "Administrator sign in" as a footer text link. Build in this order:

1. Decide the notification address (a shared alias, not one person's mailbox) and a reply-time promise you can keep; this removes the no-sales-inbox gap before any code.
2. Create the leads table (name, email, message, optional company, status, created_at, notified_at) with RLS on and no anon policies, or access only via the FastAPI server credential.
3. Build the FastAPI endpoint: size and length caps, siteverify with hostname and action checks, failing closed, rate limits by IP and email, honeypot, insert, then plain-text email via Resend with the fixed-From/To and Reply-To pattern.
4. Build the /contact page: minimal form, Turnstile widget, privacy notice and next-step line beside the submit button, clear success and error states.
5. Change the hero to the "Get started" / "Contact sales" pair (44px targets, stacked on mobile), pointing at /contact, and keep "Administrator sign in" as a small text link away from the buttons.
6. Sign the processor DPAs, publish the retention period, and add the scheduled delete job and a deletion-on-request procedure.
7. Add a daily digest of unnotified or unreplied leads, because nothing guarantees an email lands; later add a small admin list page behind existing Supabase Auth with a role check.

## What the notes could not verify

The evidence is weaker than the recommendation might suggest. No independent study (NN/g, CXL, Baymard) of "Contact now" versus alternatives, or of contact-versus-demo wording, was found; all conversion figures are vendor claims. Nothing was found on form-versus-booking conversion for developer buyers, and the search for what early-stage dev-tool companies actually use for "Contact sales" returned nothing usable, so any "most teams use X" claim is a hypothesis; checking 10 to 20 competitor pages by view-source would settle it. Fetches for Supabase, Sentry, Cloudflare, Neon and Resend sites returned no usable button labels, and Vercel's contact page returned no form content, so peer layouts rest on four sites and no peer's response-time promise or admin-login placement was confirmed. The ranking of designs A, B and C, the checklist of mistakes, honeypot and rate-limit advice, and the 12-month retention suggestion are the researchers' own inference or practice, not sourced findings. Several primary pages were not retrieved: the ICO (404), OWASP's header-injection guidance, Supabase's RLS page (summary only), Web3Forms pricing (403), and Formspree's privacy page. Supabase Edge Function pricing and pg_net retry behaviour were not confirmed from official pages, and one third-party page gave conflicting limits. Basin's paid prices and data location, Tally's Turnstile compatibility, and Cal.com's embed docs and EU-region plan were not verified. Formsubmit, Getform, Typeform, Google Forms, SavvyCal, Pylon, Intercom, Zendesk and Help Scout were not researched at all. Your company's jurisdiction (UK versus EU) is unknown, and none of this is legal advice.

## Conclusion

The decision turns less on which tool is best than on which choice keeps options open: a stable /contact link, a database row as the source of truth, and one email processor. Everything else, including a calendar embed or a free CRM, can replace the page body later without touching the hero. The thinnest part of the evidence is the part most visible to visitors, the button wording, so treat "Contact sales" as a sensible default and measure it against a demo-style label once there is traffic to compare.
